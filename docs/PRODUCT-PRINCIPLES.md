# Product Principles

## The marketplace adapts to the person

Filters capture what someone explicitly asks for. Behavior can suggest what they find useful. Conversation can reveal why.

The user remains in control.

## Preference model

Preferences are separated into:

- **Must have** — hard constraints that can exclude inventory.
- **Prefer** — meaningful ranking signals.
- **Nice to have** — weak ranking signals.
- **Not for me** — explicit negative signals.
- **We noticed** — inferred preferences that should be visible and confirmable.

## Explain every recommendation

A recommendation should answer "Why am I seeing this?"

Example:

**Residence 8C — 94% match**
- Within your budget.
- 2 bedrooms.
- Kingston 6.
- Parking.
- High floor.
- Balcony.
- Slightly smaller than your preferred size.

Do not rely on an unexplained score.

## No invisible waiting

Every workflow should make four things clear:

1. What is happening?
2. What happens next?
3. Who has the ball?
4. Do I need to do anything?

## Management by exception

The management experience should surface decisions, risks, overdue work, and meaningful changes instead of forcing staff to inspect dashboards to discover problems.

## Sellers see the work

Seller experience should expose reach, saves, enquiries, viewings, feedback, offers, and recommended next actions rather than becoming a lead form that disappears into an agent inbox.
